The Book

Since February 2022, Orthodox priests and deacons have been defrocked for praying for peace. A non-denominational preacher who walked out holding a sign that read "No to War" was later found dead in a penal colony. Across Russia, believers who refused to bless the war have been silenced, stripped of their orders, and imprisoned — not only by the state, but by their own Church.

Religious Communities Under Pressure brings that record to English for the first time. It documents more than 120 believers — Orthodox, Catholic, Protestant, Jewish, and Buddhist — and traces the machinery behind their persecution: a system more intricate, and in some ways more insidious, than anything the Soviet state devised. The great majority are Orthodox Christians, the only ones pressed from two directions at once — by the state, and by a Church that has surrendered its freedom to it.

Chapnin writes from within that Church. For fifteen years he shaped the media policy of the Moscow Patriarchate. In December 2015 he warned publicly that Russia was remaking Orthodoxy into a state ideology, and that a future war against Ukraine would be given a religious justification. Within weeks, Patriarch Kirill dismissed him. Seven years later, the warning proved exact.

Who This Book Is For

Journalists and policy analysts covering Russia, religious freedom, or the war in Ukraine — the book provides eight appendices of primary-source lists (foreign agents, "undesirable" organizations, imprisoned believers) usable as direct reference.

Clergy and lay Orthodox Christians across jurisdictions — especially in the OCA, ROCOR, and the Ecumenical Patriarchate — grappling with what it means to remain in communion with the Moscow Patriarchate.

Scholars of religion, human rights, and post-Soviet politics — the first English-language study to map ecclesiastical, state, and cross-religious repression as a single system.

Government and NGO practitioners working on Countries of Particular Concern designations, sanctions, or refugee support — Chapter 9 offers targeted recommendations for U.S., EU, and ecumenical bodies.
