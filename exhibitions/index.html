Exhibitions

Beauty during Wartime and Before
2023–2025
Sheen Center, New York, USA
Refuge Gallery, Fordham University, New York, USA

Curator: Sergei Chapnin

Partners: C-Icon Art Gallery

Contemporary Ukrainian Christian art made under Russia's full-scale invasion. At its center stood Icons on Ammo Boxes by Sofia Atlantova and Oleksandr Klymenko, who paint sacred images on the wooden lids of ammunition crates abandoned by Russian forces — among them the Mariupol Deisis — with proceeds funding medical aid in Ukraine. The first showing, at the Sheen Center in Manhattan (2023), set these icons alongside the calligraphy of Oleksiy Chekal, the gouaches of Yurii Khymych, and works by Anna Ravliuc; the second, at Fordham University's Refuge Gallery (2024–2025) under the title Wartime Beauty, was given to icons alone.

Saints of the Undivided Church
2017–2021
Touring:
National Art Museum of the Republic of Belarus, Minsk, Belarus
Valamon luostari, Heinävesi, Finland
Kuzbass Museum of Fine Arts, Novokuznetsk, Russia
Yeltsin Center, Ekaterinburg, Russia

Curators: Sergei Chapnin, Irina Yazykova

Contemporary icons of the Western saints venerated before the schism of 1054 — figures still honored by the Orthodox and Catholic churches alike. The project gathered some 120 icons by iconographers from fifteen countries and, over several years, travelled from its premiere at the National Art Museum of Belarus in Minsk (2017) to the Valamo Monastery in Finland, Novokuznetsk, and the Yeltsin Center in Yekaterinburg (2021).

Pour la Vie du Monde
14–20 January 2020
Collège des Bernardins, Paris, France

Curators: Sergei Chapnin, Irina Yazykova, Antoine Arjakovsky

Contemporary Orthodox icons from Russia, Ukraine, Belarus, and beyond, shown in the former sacristy of the Collège des Bernardins during the January week of prayer for Christian unity. Under the Russian title За жизнь мира, the exhibition set out to present icon painting not as a petrified tradition but as a living space of spiritual and artistic search.

Snowy Christmas
23 December 2019–9 January 2020
Antique Center on Sadovaya, Moscow, Russia

Curators: Irina Yazykova, Lyudmila Nenasheva, Sergei Chapnin

Contemporary Christian art on the theme of the Nativity — painting, sculpture, and ceramics rather than icons alone — gathered in central Moscow across the Christmas season, bringing established artists together with younger names.

At the Crossroads of Time and Eternity
2019
Museum of the Tkachev Brothers, Bryansk, Russia
Tambov Regional Museum of Local Lore, Tambov, Russia

Curator: Irina Yazykova

Organizer: Sergei Chapnin

Subtitled Contemporary Icon Painters of Russia and Belarus, the project brought together current Orthodox iconography from both countries. It was shown in Bryansk, at the Museum of the Tkachev Brothers within the Slavic arts festival there, and in Tambov.

Mount Athos
2016
Aleksandrovskaya Sloboda Museum-Reserve, Alexandrov, Vladimir Oblast, Russia

Curator: Sergei Chapnin

A photographic project by Vladimir Orlov, drawn from the monastic republic of Mount Athos — its churches, its landscape, and the daily rhythm of ascetic life. Shown at the Aleksandrovskaya Sloboda museum-reserve.

Contemporary Icon Painters of Russia
10 December 2015–January 2016
Tsarskaya Bashnya Gallery, Kazansky Railway Station, Moscow, Russia

Curators: Sergei Chapnin, Irina Yazykova

The first large-scale exhibition of contemporary icon painting, organized by the Artos Fellowship together with Valaam Monastery. Around a hundred works by some sixty living iconographers — more than half shown in public for the first time — filled the Tsarskaya Bashnya gallery in Moscow's Kazansky station, gathered as the seed of a planned icon museum at Valaam.

Светолитие / Svetolit'e
10 October–13 December 2015
Aleksandrovskaya Sloboda Museum-Reserve, Alexandrov, Vladimir Oblast, Russia

Curators: Sergei Chapnin, Irina Yazykova

A pairing of two artists who speak a contemporary language while staying close to the icon tradition: the religious portraits of Alla Ponyatovskaya and the panel sculpture of Sergei Nekrasov. Its title, Svetolitie — a coinage on svet, ‘light’ — takes its cue from the Gospel: “if your eye be light, your whole body will be full of light.” Shown in the exhibition hall of the Dormition Cathedral at the Aleksandrovskaya Sloboda museum-reserve.

The Gifts (Дары)
22 December 2013–19 January 2014
Shchusev State Museum of Architecture, Moscow, Russia

Curators: Sergei Chapnin, Gor Chakhal

An early project of the newly formed Artos Fellowship, and the source of its name — the almanac The Gifts (Дары) grew out of this exhibition, and the fellowship's exhibition work developed from it. Under the full title The Gifts: Art for Christmas, it set church and secular artists side by side, believers and non-believers, around a single question: what are the gifts a person brings to God. Traditional iconography, fresco, and mosaic met contemporary installation in the Ruina wing of Moscow's Shchusev Museum of Architecture.

Seven Lines from Virgil's Fourth Eclogue
2013
Pokrovskie Vorota Cultural Center, Moscow, Russia

Curator: Sergei Chapnin

The first project of the Artos Fellowship: calligraphy by Oleksiy Chekal on seven lines from the Fourth Eclogue of Virgil — the verses that Christian readers long took as a pagan foretelling of the birth of Christ.
